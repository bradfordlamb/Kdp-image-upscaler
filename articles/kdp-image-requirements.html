KDP Image Requirements for Print Books

KDP's print requirements for images are published in two help topics: the paperback submission guidelines, and the page titled Format Images in Your Book. This guide collects the image-related rules from those pages and shows how to apply them to a file you still have time to fix. It is a reading aid. The help topics themselves are what KDP scores your upload against, and they change.

Hardcover books are mentioned on the image page alongside paperbacks. The numbered specifications below are the paperback guidelines page unless noted. If you are printing a hardcover, open the current hardcover topic as well. Do not assume every paperback number is copied there.

Resolution

All images in the cover and in the manuscript should be at least 300 DPI. The image page recommends a minimum of 300 DPI, says images at 300 DPI produce the best print quality, and defines low resolution as anything under 200 DPI. The guidelines page adds a recommended maximum of 600 DPI so the file stays under 650 MB. The image page says there is no set maximum, and that excessively high resolutions can time out or delay manufacturing. The practical reading is: at least 300 DPI at the size the image is printed, and avoid climbing past 600 DPI on a book that is already a large PDF.

DPI here means pixels divided by the placed inches. A properties field that says 300 DPI does not satisfy the rule if the pixel dimensions are tiny. KDP says altering that field does not add pixels. The DPI guide shows the multiplication for common placements, including images that sit inside the margin box rather than covering the whole trim.

File type, file size, and what must not be in the PDF

  • Each of the cover upload and the interior upload must be 650 MB or smaller.
  • File names must not contain emoji or other special characters KDP does not support.
  • Embed fonts and images before you submit. Flatten transparent objects and layers. KDP lists unflattened transparency among the common reasons a file fails, because transparent regions can print as a different color than the screen shows.
  • Do not include crop marks, trim marks, bookmarks, comments, invisible objects, annotations, placeholder text, or metadata they have called out as disallowed. Do not leave a PDF-tool logo or watermark on the page.
  • A manuscript with images or elements that bleed must be a PDF. A manuscript with no bleed may be PDF, DOC, DOCX, RTF, HTML, or TXT. KDP converts the non-PDF types to PDF.
  • A custom cover is a PDF. If you build the cover in Cover Creator instead, that tool accepts JPG, PNG, and GIF.
  • Pages must be single pages, not printer spreads. Every page has to be rotated the same way.

For the images inside those files, PNG is the better container when the picture has type, icons, or hard edges. JPEG is the better container for photographs, saved once at high quality. The comparison is in PNG vs JPG. This site's upscaler writes PNG or JPEG only. It does not write the TIFF files some other printers ask for, and it does not build the cover PDF.

Bleed and page size

Cover bleed is 0.125 inch (3.2 mm) on all four sides, and the bottom bleed must not exceed 0.125 inch. Interior bleed, when you use it, extends 0.125 inch past the trim on the top, bottom, and outer edge. It does not extend into the gutter. KDP asks you to format that interior PDF 0.125 inch wider and 0.25 inch taller than the trim. A 6 × 9 inch bleed interior page is therefore 6.125 × 9.25 inches. At 300 DPI that is 1,838 × 2,775 pixels after you round the half pixel up.

Type and other content you need to keep should be at least 0.25 inch (6.4 mm) from the outside edge of a cover. Interior margins are a separate table. The inside (gutter) minimum runs from 0.375 inch at 24–150 pages up to 0.875 inch at 701–828 pages. The outside minimum is 0.25 inch with no bleed and 0.375 inch with bleed. Full rows are in the DPI guide and on the guidelines page. An image placed across the text block has to be measured against that block, not against the trim, or the DPI number will look better than the print.

Color, grayscale, and line weight

Color photographs print in color only if you selected a color ink option. A black-and-white photo prints in black and white whether or not you paid for color ink. That sentence is from the image-formatting page, and it surprises people who expected a color interior to colorize a grayscale scan. It will not.

If you selected black ink on white or cream paper and the design uses gray backgrounds, KDP recommends a grayscale fill of at least 10 percent. Rules, charts, and graphic lines need a minimum thickness of 0.75 point, which they also state as 0.01 inch (0.3 mm). Hairlines that look fine on a laptop fall out on press. Interior body text has a minimum size of 7 points, and those fonts have to be embedded. A picture of text is not a substitute for embedded type. If the words are inside an image, they need enough pixels to stay legible, which is the 300 DPI floor applied to that image, and 600 DPI is the case discussed for line art in the 300 vs 600 guide.

Covers, spine text, and barcodes

The cover is one continuous image centered on the spine. Spine width is page count times a paper factor: 0.002252 inch for white, 0.0025 inch for cream, 0.00235 inch for groundwood, and 0.002347 inch for color. Spine text is printed only on books with more than 79 pages, with at least 0.0625 inch between the text and the spine edge, plus another 0.0625 inch of binding variance on the folds. You can supply a barcode or let KDP add one to the back cover. The cover calculator guide works a full example and points at KDP's downloadable template, which is the artboard to use for the real file.

The front cover needs a title. KDP lists a missing front-cover title, a missing barcode, and missing pages among common failures. The ebook marketing cover is a different image and must not include the spine, the barcode, or the back cover. See the Kindle cover guide.

How to check a file you already have

  1. Read pixel width and height from the image, not from a DPI dropdown.
  2. Divide by the inches that image occupies in the PDF, including bleed if it bleeds.
  3. If the result is under 300, the file does not meet the stated minimum. A better source or a smaller placement is KDP's preferred repair. Resampling up to the pixel count is a fallback, and it will not add detail. The tool on this site does that fallback in the browser, without uploading the image.
  4. Confirm the PDF is flattened, fonts are embedded, and there are no crop marks.
  5. Confirm each upload is under 650 MB and the file name is plain.
  6. Read KDP's previewer, then order a proof for anything you cannot bear to see misprinted. The image page recommends a proof before the book is offered for sale.

The upscaler is a pixel resample, not a preflight tool. It will not find crop marks, a missing embed, or a spine that is the wrong width.
