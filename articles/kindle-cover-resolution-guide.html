Kindle Cover Resolution Guide

A Kindle ebook cover on KDP is a front-cover image for the product page and the device, not a paperback wrap. KDP publishes the requirements on two help pages, and those pages do not match on every number. This guide quotes both, says where they differ, and describes one file that sits inside the stricter end of each list. It does not pick a winner that Amazon has not picked.

The two pages are What criteria does my eBook's cover image need to meet? and Cover Image Guidelines for Kindle eBooks. Check them again before you upload. The summary below is only as current as those pages were when this article was written.

The size both pages recommend

Both pages give the same ideal dimensions: 2,560 pixels in height by 1,600 pixels in width. 2,560 ÷ 1,600 = 1.6, which is the height-to-width ratio the criteria page calls ideal: for every 1,000 pixels of width, 1,600 pixels of height. Both pages also say that for the best quality on high-definition devices the image should be 2,500 pixels tall. A file that is exactly 1,600 × 2,560 (width × height) satisfies the ideal box and clears 2,500 pixels of height.

Both pages cap each side at 10,000 pixels. Both pages cap the file at 50 MB. The criteria page says the file must be less than 50 MB. The guidelines page says the maximum is 50 MB. Stay under 50 MB either way. Both pages warn that extra compression hurts the cover when it is shown on a device or on the website, and they tell you to upload with as little compression as you can manage inside that cap.

If the image is smaller than 2,560 × 1,600, the guidelines page says you get a warning at upload. It also says Amazon strongly recommends you create a new image at the recommended size, and that you should not stretch the image to get there, because the stretch lowers quality. A resample done properly is still interpolation. It is closer to a real rebuild than a stretch in a layout box, and it is still worse than art that was designed at 1,600 × 2,560. If you have the layered master, export it at that size. Do not scale a screenshot of a mockup.

A practical file that does not depend on which sentence a reviewer reads first: a JPEG, RGB, 1,600 pixels wide by 2,560 pixels tall, saved once at high quality, under 50 MB. JPEG is on both format lists, and the guidelines page prefers it. The pixel size meets the ideal on both pages and is above both minimums. RGB meets the criteria page and matches the guidelines page's recommendation. You do not need to hit 72 dpi as a target. That figure is in the criteria table, and the same page, plus the guidelines page, tells you to prefer 300 PPI for quality. An image that is already 1,600 × 2,560 pixels has the samples those quality notes are asking for. Tagging it 72 or 300 does not add or remove those pixels. KDP's print documentation makes the same point about DPI tags. The ebook pages do not spell the tag issue out as clearly, so treat the pixel dimensions as the requirement you can verify.

What the image may contain

The guidelines page says the marketing cover is separate from the ebook file. It must include the title and the author or contributor. It must not include the spine, the barcode, the back cover, or an inside flap. It must not mention price or a temporary promotion. It must not infringe someone else's cover. Type has to match the detail page, including spelling of the title and author, and the language of the cover should match the language of the book. Text should be readable, not cut off, and not fading into the background. A white or very light cover can disappear on Amazon's white page. Both pages suggest a narrow medium-gray border, 3 or 4 pixels, to show the edge.

There is also an internal cover inside the ebook file. The guidelines page says quality assurance will fail the book if that interior cover is too small, that it should fill at least half of the space allotted to it, that it should match the marketing cover and the detail page, and that you should not also add an HTML cover page or the cover can appear twice. It points at the OPF manifest for how the interior cover is declared. Build the interior cover from the same master, at a large pixel size, rather than from a thumbnail.

Using the upscaler when the master is smaller

If the only file you have is smaller than 1,600 × 2,560 and you cannot re-export the design, you can resample it. The tool on this site multiplies width and height by target DPI ÷ source DPI. It keeps the aspect ratio. It will not change a square into a 1.6 ratio. Crop or redesign first if the proportions are wrong. A 1,000 × 1,600 image scaled by 1.6 becomes 1,600 × 2,560. To get a scale of 1.6 from the tool, use any source and target pair with that ratio, such as source 100 and target 160. Source 72 and target 300 would scale by 4.166… and overshoot badly. The form's defaults are for print, not for this ebook size.

The result is interpolated. Fine type gets smoother and not more detailed. The guidelines page would still prefer you create the image at the final size. The download is PNG or JPEG, with no reliable DPI tag, which is acceptable here because the check is the pixel size. Choose JPEG for a photographic cover, quality 95, RGB. If you need TIFF because you are following the criteria page strictly, export TIFF from an editor after this step. This tool does not write TIFF. Confirm the file is under 50 MB. A PNG of a busy painting at 1,600 × 2,560 can be large, but it rarely threatens 50 MB. A print wrap at 600 DPI is a different problem and belongs in the cover size guide.

Scale to 1,600 × 2,560, not to a print DPI. Pick a source and target whose ratio equals the scale you calculated from the pixels.

Do not upload the paperback wrap as the Kindle cover

A paperback PDF is one image of the back, spine, and front. The Kindle guidelines tell you the marketing image must not include the spine, barcode, or back cover. Uploading the wrap makes the front cover a strip on the product page. Export the front panel on its own, at 1,600 × 2,560, from the same master you used for the wrap. The wrap itself still has to meet the print rules: 300 DPI across the full width including spine and bleed. Those pixels are calculated in the low-resolution cover guide. The two files can share artwork. They cannot be the same JPEG.

After upload, the guidelines page says Upload History shows whether the cover failed, with a status report you can download. A failure for size or format is specific. Fix that item. Do not respond to an "image too small" error by stretching. Rebuild or resample to 1,600 × 2,560 and upload the new file.

A file you can hand to KDP

  • JPEG, RGB, no color separation.
  • 1,600 × 2,560 pixels (width × height), which is a 1.6 ratio and taller than 2,500 pixels.
  • Saved once, at high quality, under 50 MB.
  • Front cover only: title, author, no price, no barcode, no spine, no back cover.
  • A 3-to-4-pixel medium-gray edge if the background is white or nearly white.
  • A matching large interior cover inside the ebook, without a second HTML cover page.
